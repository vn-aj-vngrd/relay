# Product landing-page storytelling

Research date: August 17, 2026

## Question

How should Relay’s landing page narrate a complete recreational-sports workflow using the real product, while keeping Linear’s restraint and Apple-like product clarity?

## Primary-source observations

### Linear

- Linear’s current homepage leads with a concise category thesis, then immediately uses large product imagery rather than an abstract illustration. Its page repeatedly pairs one product claim with an interface view, letting the UI carry the evidence. [Linear homepage](https://linear.app/)
- Linear groups its value into three principles—purpose-built, agent-powered, and speed—before expanding into product workflows. The useful pattern for Relay is the hierarchy, not the content: establish why the product exists, then narrate workflows with real UI. [Linear homepage](https://linear.app/)
- Linear’s redesign explicitly targeted reduced visual noise, consistent alignment, and stronger hierarchy and density across the sidebar, tabs, headers, and panels. It describes the app chrome as an “inverted L” and says it tested environment, appearance, and hierarchy. [How we redesigned the Linear UI](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)
- Linear says it used Apple standards to move closer to native behavior across macOS, Windows, and browser environments. It also emphasizes that label, icon, and button alignment is something users feel rather than immediately notice. [How we redesigned the Linear UI](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)

### GitHub

- GitHub’s Features page organizes a broad platform into outcome-led chapters such as collaborative coding, automation, security, and client apps. It avoids presenting every capability as an equal card; related details sit under a clear workflow heading. [GitHub Features](https://github.com/features)
- Each GitHub chapter combines a direct outcome statement with concrete product capabilities and links. For Relay, the equivalent is to group details under Plan, Organize, Pay, Play, and Remember rather than making a flat feature wall. [GitHub Features](https://github.com/features)

### Apple

- Apple’s iPhone page pairs benefit-led copy with specific product imagery—for example, a Continuity benefit is shown with the actual iPhone and Mac interfaces involved. The image demonstrates the claim instead of decorating it. [Apple iPhone](https://www.apple.com/iphone/)
- Apple’s sections stay focused on one user-visible outcome at a time. Relay should apply that discipline to “the invite answers everything,” “everyone knows who is next,” and “the split stays clear.” [Apple iPhone](https://www.apple.com/iphone/)

## Relay direction

1. Use screenshots captured from the current Relay application, not invented dashboard mockups.
2. Put the complete session workspace directly beneath the hero thesis.
3. Narrate the real sequence: create and share → roster and booking → payment → courts and queue → chat and notifications → memory and Play Again.
4. Give each major section one claim, one short explanation, and one product image.
5. Use a compact capability index for secondary features instead of repeated icon cards.
6. Preserve Linear’s density, alignment, neutral surfaces, and quiet chrome while retaining Relay’s court field, optic ball, and score typography.
7. Keep the landing page light-first and mostly white. Product screenshots provide the visual richness.
8. State the recreational positioning positively: Relay is the shared home for pickleball with friends. Ratings, leagues, and public feeds do not need to dominate the page.

## Content hierarchy

- Hero: one shared session from invite to final score.
- Product proof: full authenticated session workspace.
- Thesis: one session carries the entire night.
- Invite: account-optional link and guest RSVP.
- Organize/pay: roster, booking readiness, and host-paid collection.
- Play: Paddle Stack, Mix It Up, Court Climb, queue, scoring, and standings.
- Stay together: realtime chat, photos, notifications.
- Return: memories, groups, and Play Again.
- Capability index and final CTA.
