# Research: Relay browser-only Story motion and social exports

## Summary
Keep the existing **1080×1920 PNG export** as the dependable baseline. Animation is feasible entirely in-browser, but the realistic social-delivery candidate is a short **H.264 MP4**, offered only where recording and real-device import have been verified—not a universally supported GIF, universal browser-native MP4, or direct Instagram posting feature.

**Research timing:** Sources were retrieved live during this research run; the runtime did not expose an authoritative calendar date. Version/date anchors below are explicit rather than an invented access date. This is documentation research, not browser/account testing. No project files, dependencies, credentials, or accounts were used or modified.

## Findings

1. **“GIFs work on Stories” needs qualification.** Facebook’s consumer Help Center explicitly includes `.gif` among uploadable video formats and recommends **MP4 or MOV**. That establishes general Facebook upload support, not guaranteed animated playback through every Facebook Story picker, feed surface, or browser share target. Instagram’s consumer Story help documents photos and videos; indexed official sticker help documents in-app stickers. Neither establishes reliable import of a locally generated animated `.gif` as an Instagram Story or feed animation. In-app GIF stickers are not equivalent to uploading your own GIF file. Therefore avoid both blanket claims: “GIF always works” and “Facebook never accepts GIF.” [Facebook upload formats](https://www.facebook.com/help/218673814818907) · [Instagram Story editing](https://www.facebook.com/help/instagram/314684928883274) · [Instagram stickers—search evidence only; full extraction failed](https://www.facebook.com/help/151273688993748/)

2. **Native sharing, Graph API publishing, ads, and organic consumer upload are different contracts.** Meta’s Instagram **native Android/iOS Sharing to Stories** integration opens the composer for the user to publish; it uses Android intents/iOS URL schemes and requires a Facebook App ID. Its Android background-asset table lists JPG/PNG and H.264/H.265/WebM video, minimum 720×1280, recommended 9:16 or 9:18, and up to 20 seconds. Those are native-integration specifications—not browser Web Share promises. Instagram Content Publishing instead targets professional accounts, tokens and permissions, with `STORIES` containers. Its URL-based flow fetches public media; the current guide also documents resumable binary video uploads, so “every API upload requires public hosting” is too broad. Either flow is outside this private, no-integration export scope. Ads acceptance must not be used to infer consumer GIF support; no ads limits are adopted here. [Native Sharing to Stories](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/sharing-to-stories) · [Content Publishing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/content-publishing/)

3. **Canvas → MediaStream → MediaRecorder is the smallest native animation path.** `canvas.captureStream(frameRate)` supplies one video track; it does not require camera or microphone capture. Render motion into the canvas itself—CSS animation around the canvas is not part of its recorded bitmap. A tainted canvas throws `SecurityError`. `MediaRecorder.isTypeSupported()` is necessary but not sufficient: recording can still fail because of resources or other runtime conditions. Catch construction, start, recording, finalization and playback failures. [Canvas captureStream](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/HTMLCanvasElement/captureStream) · [Recorder capability detection](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/MediaRecorder/isTypeSupported_static) · [WebKit MediaRecorder](https://webkit.org/blog/11353/mediarecorder-api/)

4. **Do not encode browser-family assumptions into the product.** WebKit’s initial iOS 14.3-era announcement documented MP4/H.264/AAC recording and canvas-generated inputs. Safari **18.4** expanded recording to WebM/VP8/VP9/Opus and additional MP4 codecs. Chromium’s **Chrome 136 beta** notes explicitly describe MP4 H26x MediaRecorder support updates. Thus “Safari only MP4, Chrome only WebM” is stale, while “all modern browsers export MP4” is also unjustified. Probe H.264-in-MP4 MIME candidates on the actual device. MP4 is a container, not a codec guarantee; generic `video/mp4` can conceal a different encoder choice. Prefer an explicitly accepted AVC/H.264 configuration and inspect the recorder’s actual MIME type. Use the actual format for Blob/File MIME and filename; **renaming WebM to `.mp4` is not conversion**. [WebKit initial implementation](https://webkit.org/blog/11353/mediarecorder-api/) · [Safari 18.4](https://webkit.org/blog/16574/webkit-features-in-safari-18-4/) · [Chrome 136 beta](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/chrome-136-beta)

5. **Web Share is an optional file handoff, not a posting API.** Feature-detect `navigator.share` and `navigator.canShare({files:[file]})` using the actual generated file, in HTTPS. Browser file support and installed share targets vary; successful handoff does not establish publication or guarantee Instagram/Facebook appears, accepts the file, or opens its Story composer. Sharing requires transient user activation: finish asynchronous encoding first, then present a separate **Share file** button whose click calls `share()` directly. Keep **Download PNG** / **Download video** available, and treat share cancellation as cancellation rather than an export error. [Navigator.share](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Navigator/share) · [Web Share overview](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Web_Share_API)

6. **GIF is possible, but adds work without solving social compatibility.** `gifenc` is a small pure-JS encoder with palette quantization, worker examples and MIT licensing; its author says it is better suited to flat vector art than photographs, with no dithering support. Its quoted Chrome benchmark is an author benchmark, not evidence for Relay on iPhones. The `gif.js` repository is also MIT licensed—do not assume GPL from its name. Retain copyright/license notices and review the exact selected package/transitive code if adopted. Full-HD frame storage is expensive: one 1080×1920 RGBA frame is 8,294,400 bytes (~7.9 MiB); retaining 90 frames is ~712 MiB before quantization, encoding buffers or photos. A future GIF option should use a worker, bounded frame queues, lower resolution/frame rate and explicit limits. WebCodecs/MP4 muxing or WASM transcoding introduces another implementation/resource/licensing surface and is not the smallest phase-one path. [gifenc README](https://github.com/mattdesl/gifenc) · [gifenc MIT license](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mattdesl/gifenc/master/LICENSE.md) · [gif.js MIT license](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jnordberg/gif.js/master/LICENSE) · [Chrome WebCodecs guide](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/web-platform/best-practices/webcodecs)

7. **Original motifs suit the privacy and asset constraints.** Draw original pickleball paddles, visibly perforated balls, court lines, ribbon bows, scallops and torn tape using canvas paths or self-contained SVG; avoid external image/font references in SVG. This supplies actual recognizable art rather than palette-only themes, without importing a third-party motif asset license. It also avoids external-asset canvas taint; other inputs still need review. User photos can be decoded from local Files/object URLs, drawn and exported in memory. Existing remote photos require same-origin access or correctly authorized CORS; setting `crossOrigin` alone is insufficient. Font licenses and user-photo rights still apply. [Canvas CORS/security](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/How_to/CORS_enabled_image)

## Recommended phase-one implementation scope

These are product recommendations, not claims about current visual trends or inspected Relay source code.

### Baseline: ship creative static export first

- Preserve the current 1080×1920 PNG path and its behavior; treat every animation capability as optional enhancement.
- Provide **at least five genuinely different compositions**, with pink prominent and original pickleball art across the set: **Pink Coquette** (ribbon bows/scallops/paddles), **Courtside Club** (pink court diagram/scoreboard), **Scrapbook Rally** (torn tape/photo layers/ball doodles), **Retro Matchday** (sunburst/checker accents/paddle badge), and **Clean Editorial** (large type/airy pink/outlined equipment). These are proposed creative directions, not researched trend rankings.
- Keep scores, session information and faces readable; do not let decorations obscure essential content. Reuse the same canvas scene renderer for preview, still export and optional animation where feasible.
- Static preview by default. Offer **Preview animation** and Stop; no autoplay on theme selection. Honor `prefers-reduced-motion` in both CSS and JS/canvas scheduling. Reduced-motion users retain still export and may explicitly request a bounded animation; do not silently restart it. Avoid flashing and keep text/photo positions stable. [Reduced-motion reference](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-reduced-motion)

### Optional, gated motion slice

1. Only after a user chooses **Create video**, decode local assets, await font readiness, draw the first frame and start bounded canvas recording. Proposed initial target: **3 seconds, 30 fps, silent, 1080×1920**, with subtle motif motion. These are engineering targets—not Meta minimums or guaranteed device capacity.
2. Check `captureStream`, `MediaRecorder`, and explicit MP4/AVC support; use a correctly supported H.264 codec/profile string rather than demanding an audio codec for a stream with no audio. Generic MP4 success alone is not proof of H.264 output.
3. Record in real time; constrain duration and concurrent jobs, provide cancel/progress, and handle backgrounding/interruption. Stop tracks, cancel animation callbacks, discard temporary chunks and revoke object URLs after use. Do not retain an array of full-resolution raw frames.
4. On completion, require a nonempty Blob and successful local video metadata/playback preview before exposing Download/Share. This checks basic output health, **not** Instagram/Facebook acceptance or exact codec conformance.
5. Offer H.264 MP4 only on device/browser combinations subsequently verified with actual files and native app imports. If universal animated export is a launch requirement, this native-recorder slice cannot honestly satisfy it without additional engineering.
6. If MP4 is unsupported or export fails, keep the original PNG available with: **“Video export isn’t available on this device. Download a still image instead.”** No automatic server upload/transcode. WebM may later be a clearly labeled general-purpose download, but is not the default cross-platform Story fallback. Do not ship a GIF encoder or WebCodecs/WASM stack in the smallest first release.
7. Share copy: **“Share file”**, not “Post to Instagram.” Download guidance: **“Save the file, then add it in Instagram or Facebook.”** State that the user chooses the destination and completes posting.

### Privacy boundary

New creative processing can remain local: File/object URL → decoded photo → canvas → PNG or recorded Blob → download/native share. Do not send photos, canvas pixels, generated blobs or filenames to analytics, conversion services or third-party asset endpoints; avoid persistence by default. “Local export” does not mean Relay never makes network requests, nor that the selected social app keeps shared content private. Existing photo retrieval/storage is outside this uninspected research scope.

## Gaps and next steps

- No exhaustive public consumer contract was established for arbitrary animated GIF import into Instagram Stories/feed or Facebook Stories. Facebook general GIF upload support does not resolve placement-specific behavior. Verify in the real apps before marketing a GIF feature.
- Browser versions, iOS in-app browsers, hardware encoders, silent MP4 acceptance, fragmented MP4 handling, save-to-Photos versus Files behavior and available share targets remain untested. Do not guarantee universal MP4, audio handling, duration/frame accuracy or successful posting from documentation alone.
- A later authorized test matrix should include current/minimum-supported iPhone Safari, Android Chrome and desktop Safari/Chrome; PNG, generated silent H.264 MP4 and an unsupported-recorder path; native Instagram/Facebook Story and feed imports; cancellation, background interruption, low-memory behavior and reduced-motion. Never infer publication from a resolved Web Share promise.
- No source inspection or implementation validation was performed. **Validation deferred to pre-commit.**
