# Mobile social story patterns — September 2026

**Question:** How should Relay structure mobile-first story creation, customization, sharing, conversion, privacy, and lifecycle content?

**Method:** First-party product help and developer documentation only, reviewed 2026-09-04. The note describes documented behavior rather than inferring controls from third-party screenshots.

## What leading apps do

| Product | Documented interaction pattern | Lifecycle, audience, and conversion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Instagram / Meta** | Stories begin with a photo or video, then expose customization as a second layer. Instagram's documented sticker set includes Link, location, mention, music, poll, questions, countdown, captions, and more; the Link sticker accepts a URL, offers a destination preview, and is tappable by anyone who can see the Story ([Instagram Help — stickers](https://www.facebook.com/help/instagram/151273688993748/)). Meta's native share integration likewise sends a background and/or transparent sticker into Instagram's composer, where the user can continue editing; it recommends a vertical 9:16 asset ([Meta — Sharing to Stories](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/sharing-to-stories/)). | A Story disappears from profile, Feed, and messages after 24 hours unless promoted into a durable Highlight ([Instagram Help — Stories](https://www.facebook.com/help/instagram/1660923094227526), [visibility duration](https://www.facebook.com/help/instagram/1729008150678239/)). Audience is explicit: Close Friends is a send target, and creators can hide future Stories from selected people without blocking them ([Close Friends](https://www.facebook.com/help/instagram/2183694401643300/), [hide a Story](https://www.facebook.com/help/instagram/1177797265575168/)). |
| **TikTok** | The Story path starts with **Photo, Text, or a video duration**, followed by capture/upload and then filters, sound, text, stickers, settings, and advanced video editing. This is progressive disclosure rather than an all-at-once canvas ([TikTok Support — TikTok Stories](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/tiktok-stories)). The broader editor supports templates, clip ordering/trimming, multi-track editing, sound, transitions, text duration, overlays, and preview-before-confirm flows ([TikTok Support — editing videos and photos](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/editing-tiktok-videos-and-photos)). | Stories remain visible for 24 hours and can appear on profile, inbox, Following, and For You; viewers can react or reply ([TikTok Stories](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/tiktok-stories)). | **Snapchat** | The camera is the starting surface. Links are attached from the **Preview** screen with a paperclip, then the creator pastes, types, or searches for a URL before choosing **Attach to Snap** ([Snapchat Support — attach a link](https://help.snapchat.com/hc/en-us/articles/7012382154900-How-do-I-attach-a-link-to-a-Snap)). Distribution is separated into a later **Send To** choice. | Public and friends-only Stories are visibly separate destinations; Private or Shared Stories ask who can view and contribute, then ask for a name ([public vs friends](https://help.snapchat.com/hc/en-us/articles/7012338216468-What-s-the-difference-between-posting-to-My-Story-Public-and-My-Story-Friends), [Private/Shared Stories](https://help.snapchat.com/hc/en-us/articles/7012343640084-How-to-Create-a-Private-or-Shared-Story-on-Snapchat)). ## Transferable interaction patterns

1. **Media or truth first; decoration second.** Instagram, TikTok, Snapchat, Strava, and Spotify all begin from a camera/upload or an existing content object. Relay should begin from the authoritative game state and strongest available photo, immediately show the composed preview, then reveal customization.
2. **Progress through small decisions.** A strong mobile sequence is **focus → look → optional details → audience/destination**. TikTok's capture-then-edit flow and Snapchat's Preview-then-Send-To flow keep distribution decisions out of the creative surface.
3. **Use tabs/chip rails only for bounded, peer choices.** A horizontally scrollable rail fits Relay's lifecycle-valid focuses and themes. Crop, contrast, copy, privacy, and share actions are not peer “styles”; keep them in contextual sheets or a short settings step. The useful model is a few understandable alternatives over a freeform canvas.
4. **Keep the preview dominant and honest.** Spotify previews the destination appearance; Strava overlays only supported statistics; Meta hands content into the destination composer for final edits. Relay's preview should match export, label live snapshots with time, and never let style controls alter game facts.
5. **Separate artifact lifetime from social-channel lifetime.** Instagram Highlights and Snapchat Memories preserve selected ephemeral moments; Strava keeps the underlying activity durable. Relay should keep a durable canonical game/story artifact even when an exported Story disappears after 24 hours elsewhere.
6. **Put share actions together, but preserve distinct jobs.** Native share/destination, copy link, download, and QR belong in one completion sheet. They are not interchangeable: an image earns attention, a link converts, a QR serves in-person or second-screen handoff, and download is a resilience fallback.
7. **Permission precedes publication.** Snapchat makes the audience a send destination; TikTok and Strava expose item-level visibility; Strava suppresses sharing when visibility forbids it. Relay should compute eligible audiences/actions from game policy and actor permissions before rendering public links or QR.
8. **Attribution should enable return, not dominate the card.** Spotify's content shares and Strava's stat cards remain recognizable while opening the exact source object. Relay needs restrained Relay branding plus one canonical, lifecycle-aware destination—not a large watermark and a separate generic homepage URL.

## Relay recommendations

- Open Story on a near-finished, media-first preview generated from authoritative session state. Default by lifecycle: **invitation** before play, **live update** during play, **recap/crew** after completion.
- Use a sticky vertical preview with one compact chip rail each for **Focus** and **Theme**; open crop/contrast and short copy in focused bottom sheets. Keep layout automatic unless testing proves a separate layout rail is understandable.
- Make templates phase-aware and bounded. Hide unsupported focuses rather than showing disabled or zero-value cards; allow style changes, never edits to scores, roster, price, venue, or status.
- Finish with one share sheet containing **Share**, **Copy link**, **Show QR**, and **Download**. Preview the exported asset before handoff and make the link/QR resolve to the exact public artifact or eligible RSVP action.
- Keep published artifacts durable and versioned. A live share should be an immutable “as of” snapshot; the canonical game can continue changing. A completed recap should remain available after social Stories expire.
- Show audience and exposed facts immediately above Publish/Share. Default private/draft games to no public artifact, link, or QR. Derive roster names, location precision, and stats from existing visibility rules.
- Track the complete loop—composer opened, export/share attempted, link opened, RSVP or next-game action—not only downloads. Preserve source attribution on every exported asset and deep link.

## Anti-patterns

- A desktop-style freeform canvas with dragging, layers, font pickers, and dozens of simultaneous controls.
- Theme, layout, focus, lifecycle, and audience mixed into one long chip rail.
- Requiring customization before showing a usable default preview.
- Exporting an attractive image with no tappable/copyable conversion path, or placing a QR where it cannot be scanned (for example, the same phone without a save/share fallback).
- Publishing live content that silently updates, fabricates a result, exposes provisional standings, or reveals private roster/location/payment facts.
- Treating “24-hour Story” as deletion of Relay's source artifact, or treating every durable recap as automatically public.
- Sending directly to an external app without a final Relay preview, explicit audience state, failure fallback, and user-initiated handoff.
- Oversized Relay branding, generic homepage links, or multiple competing calls to action that weaken the game-specific next step.
